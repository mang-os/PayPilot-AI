AI agents can discover products and make purchase decisions, but giving an AI unrestricted control over money creates a serious trust problem.

PayPilot AI enables agent-to-agent commerce while keeping financial execution under deterministic control.

The buyer provides a single intent: find and buy two ANC earbuds under six thousand rupees.

The buyer agent communicates with the merchant system, discovers eligible products, evaluates the constraints, selects the best candidate, applies the available offer, and automatically constructs the cart.

But the agent cannot directly move money.

Before payment, PayPilot validates merchant policy, delegated spending authority, and inventory.

Only after every financial control passes is Razorpay invoked. Payment verification and webhook evidence are stored in the transaction audit trail.

Now the agent is given a spending authority of five thousand rupees.

The requested transaction exceeds that authority, so the deterministic mandate rejects the purchase.

Inventory is not reserved, Razorpay is never invoked, payment is never attempted, and zero rupees move.

PayPilot AI enables autonomous commerce without giving autonomous AI unrestricted financial authority.
